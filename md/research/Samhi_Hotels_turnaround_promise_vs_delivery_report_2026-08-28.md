# SAMHI Hotels Ltd: A Turnaround That Delivered, Priced Like It Hasn't

## 1. Company Snapshot

SAMHI owns and turns around branded business hotels (38 hotels, 6,500+ rooms, 14 cities) under Marriott, IHG and Hyatt flags; it owns the real estate, the chains supply the guests through loyalty programmes and reservation systems.

**Growth thesis type:** turnaround + deleveraging + inorganic asset rebranding and capacity addition, anchored by the W Hyderabad opening (Q4 FY27), the ACIC portfolio integration, and the GIC upscale platform.

## Verdict

- **Trajectory:** mid-teens top-line growth is credible on the delivery record, driven by operating leverage and the W Hyderabad launch; deleveraging is real but slower than guided.
- **Key trigger:** W Hyderabad commissioning in Q4 FY27, adding ~6-7% to revenue in FY28.
- **Valuation:** the headline 6.1x P/E is an artefact of a one-time deferred tax asset; on EV/EBITDA (11.8x) SAMHI trades at a ~21% discount to the peer median (15.0x), a real but not extreme discount.
- **Management rating: Adequate.** Solid operating execution and a genuine turnaround, offset by a zero-promoter, PE-backed structure and a record of timeline slippage on key capex.

### Quantified read

- **Clean hit rate is modest:** 4 of 12 commitments delivered outright, 3 on track, 3 missed, 2 partial, 1 too early. The misses cluster in renovation timelines (Caspia, Four Points Pune) and inventory growth, not in the core operating metrics.
- **The one number that matters held:** the 9-11% same-store RevPAR guidance has been defended "pretty much from the day of IPO till today" [Q3 FY26 concall], and FY26 delivered 9.5% despite four disruption events.
- **Deleveraging is the honest gap:** guided to 2.5x in 12-18 months from Q4 FY26, but net debt/EBITDA was 3.0x at FY26 end and 3.2x at Q1 FY27, with management itself flagging 2.4x only on operating assets excluding growth capex.

## 3. Future of the Company

### Management guidance (label as such)

- Same-store RevPAR CAGR of 9-11% for 3-5 years; total revenue to reach ₹3,000 Cr by FY30.
- Cumulative EBITDA of ₹3,000-3,500 Cr over the next 4-5 years; cumulative free cash of >₹3,000 Cr over FY27-FY31.
- Net debt/EBITDA to 2.5x by FY28; upscale revenue share from ~43% to ~60% by FY30.

### Skeptical analyst view

- **Mid-teens top-line growth is the realistic base case**, not the 17-18% CAGR guided: FY26 delivered 12.3% reported (16-17% ex-disruptions), and W Hyderabad adds ~6-7% in FY28, so mid-teens is achievable without heroic assumptions.
- **Deleveraging will be steady, not fast:** the 2.5x target keeps slipping (3.5x FY25 → 2.5x "12-18 months" → FY28), and the ₹2,200 Cr capex pipeline means net debt stays elevated even as EBITDA grows.
- **The growth is back-loaded and execution-dependent:** W Hyderabad (Q4 FY27) is the only near-term catalyst; Navi Mumbai (FY31), Noida, Chennai and Hyderabad Financial District all land FY29-FY31, so the next 2-3 years rest heavily on one hotel.

### Top 3 thesis-breaking risks

- **Corporate travel slowdown / IT sluggishness** in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune, which drive the bulk of asset income.
  - *Leading indicator:* same-store occupancy and corporate negotiated ADR trends each quarter.
- **Execution / timeline slippage on key capex**, especially W Hyderabad completion.
  - *Leading indicator:* capital work-in-progress additions and management milestone updates.
- **Interest rate / refinancing drag** on a still-levered balance sheet.
  - *Leading indicator:* net debt/EBITDA ratio and the quarterly finance-cost run-rate.

## 4. Growth Triggers and Industry

### Specific triggers with timing

- **W Hyderabad (170 rooms, Q4 FY27):** expected to add ~6-7% revenue on the current run rate in FY28, at revenues per key upwards of ₹60 lakh.
- **ACIC portfolio sweating:** the transition from Marriott franchise to managed is complete; focus has shifted to market penetration and revenue growth, with margins now above 40%.
- **Bangalore / upper-midscale demand recovery:** mid-scale RevPAR grew 13.7% in Q1 FY27, and the underperforming FY26 portfolio is recovering.
- **RARE India / Marriott Outdoor Collection:** ~40 of 75 hotels signed up, with 15 pilot properties targeted to go live on Marriott.com in H2 FY27; management targets ₹35-40 Cr EBITDA in 1.5-2 years.
- **Navi Mumbai (700 rooms, FY31):** the largest project, guided to ₹180-185 Cr EBITDA at stabilization, but five years away.

### Industry read

- **Cyclical position:** a mature upcycle. Occupancy is near structural peak (79.3% portfolio, 36% of days above 90%), so growth is shifting from rate-led to occupancy-led with ADR growth moderating to ~2.6% in Q1 FY27.
- **Competitive intensity:** midscale/upscale is crowded, but SAMHI's Tier-1 brand partnerships (Marriott 66% of asset income) and institutional asset-management contracts are a genuine moat versus asset-heavy peers.
- **Position vs peers:** SAMHI is the asset-lightest of the three (P/B 1.6x vs 5.3-6.2x) and the most leveraged, which is the flip side of its turnaround profile.

## 5. Valuation vs History and Peers

| **Peer median** | **36.5x** | **15.0x** | **5.3x** | **0.68** |

*Source: Latest market data.*

### Why the headline P/E is misleading

- FY26 PAT of ₹567 Cr exceeded operating EBITDA of ₹558 Cr because it was inflated by a ~₹300 Cr deferred tax asset recognition, an ~₹83 Cr impairment reversal, and a gain on sale of Caspia Delhi.
- Normalize for these one-offs and the earnings power is roughly EBITDA minus finance costs and depreciation, which is why EV/EBITDA (11.8x) is the honest multiple, not P/E.
- **Does the price pay for delivery not yet shown?** At 11.8x vs a 15.0x peer median, SAMHI is ~21% cheaper, but its growth is back-loaded (W Hyderabad FY28, Navi Mumbai FY31) and its balance sheet is the most levered. The discount is a fair price for execution risk, not a free lunch.

## 6. Management Quality

### Guidance credibility

- **Quantified hit rate:** 4 of 12 scorecard commitments delivered outright (~33%); including on-track items, ~58% are progressing. The core RevPAR guidance has been the most reliable; renovation timelines the least.

### Capital allocation

- **Where money went:** ₹960 Cr recycled over three years (4 non-core hotels at ~20x EV/EBITDA plus the GIC infusion), with ~₹650 Cr applied to debt reduction; the GIC platform (₹750 Cr for 35% of a 1,000-room upscale platform) funds growth without balance-sheet strain.
- **What it returned:** FY26 ROCE of 11.4% is below the mid-teens target, though mature assets deliver 17-18%; the RARE India bet (₹47 Cr for 70%) is small and asset-light.

### Candor

- **Owns some misses, deflects others:** management openly explained the Caspia sale underwriting (couldn't justify the ₹12-14 Cr EBITDA needed) and quantified the four FY26 disruption events at ₹44-52 Cr of revenue. But timeline slippage on W Hyderabad and Four Points Pune is framed as "part of the plan" rather than a miss.

### Focus

- **Consistent core, one new story:** the buy-turnaround-rebrand business-hotel strategy has been stable since IPO; RARE India is the first genuine diversification, into asset-light leisure.

### Skin in the game

- **Zero promoter holding** (0.0%), a PE-backed structure; FIIs hold 44.6% and DIIs 17.4%. Chairman Manav Thadani has made small market purchases (₹0.22-0.99 Cr across 2024-2026), a token but real signal.

### Governance

- **No auditor changes flagged; dilution history is the watch item:** the IPO (Nov 2023) and the GIC minority stake issuance are the main equity events, and the GIC deal was structured to delever rather than dilute. Related-party scrutiny centres on the GIC JV and RARE transactions, both disclosed.

---

## 7. Terminal Conclusion

**The single development that would most change this view:** faster-than-expected commissioning and EBITDA contribution of W Hyderabad, accompanied by accelerated deleveraging to net debt/EBITDA below 2.0x, which would validate the back-loaded growth story and justify re-rating toward the peer median. Conversely, an unexpected sharp downturn in corporate IT business travel in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune would break the thesis at its core, since those markets drive the bulk of asset income and the entire growth pipeline is built on their continued strength.
